Final project: my personal website
The brief *My personal website.* You are going to build a small website about yourself. It is not a joke project: this is the kind of page you can send with a job application, or use as your portfolio when you finish the cycle. Write things you would really show. If you prefer not to talk about yourself, you can do it about an invented character, a band, a shop or a small business. Ask your teacher first. What it must have *Four pages, at least:*
FileContent
index.htmlHome: who you are, a photo, a short introduction
about.htmlAbout: your studies, your skills, your interests. A table or a list
projects.htmlProjects: 3 or more things you have done, with images
contact.htmlContact: a form and your links
These are the *minimum requirements*. They are the Pass level (5-6) of every row in the rubric below: if your site has all of them, you pass the project. *HTML requirements*
  • A complete skeleton in every page, with its own <title> for each one.
  • Semantic tags in the four pages: header, nav, main, section and footer.
  • The same navigation menu in the four pages, and every link working.
  • At least 5 images in an images folder, all with a descriptive alt text.
  • At least one table and one ordered or unordered list.
  • A contact form with 6 fields, all with label, and required in the compulsory ones.
  • At most 3 errors in the W3C validator, adding up the four pages.
    •  *CSS requirements*
  • One single external CSS file for the whole site.
  • No style= attributes in the HTML.
  • box-sizing: border-box.
  • A :root block with at least 5 variables.
  • A Google Font.
  • Flexbox for the navigation bar.
  • Grid or flexbox for the projects page.
  • At least one :hover effect with a transition.
  • The viewport tag and at least one media query.
  • At 375px wide: no horizontal scroll and no content cut off.
    •  *Delivery*
  • A zip file called surname-name-project.zip with the whole folder.
  • A README.txt inside with: your name, what your site is about, where you got the images, and one paragraph about what was hardest.
  • A presentation of 3 minutes in class: show your site and explain one thing you are proud of and one thing you would improve.
    •      Milestones
    StageWhat must be ready
    Stage 1The folder structure, the four HTML pages with real content and the menu working. No CSS yet.
    Stage 2The CSS file with variables, fonts, colours, the nav bar and the layout of the pages.
    Stage 3Responsive, checklist, validator, README, zip and presentation.
    Do not use images you found on Google without checking. Use your own photos, or free image sites like Unsplash, Pexels or Pixabay. Write where each image comes from in your README.     Final project rubric. Each row is linked to one criterion and gets the highest level where you meet *all* the conditions; each level includes the one before it. Insufficient means not reaching Pass. We check it by opening your site in the browser at 375, 768 and 1200 px, running the validator on the four pages and reading your code.
    Row Criterion Insufficient (1-4) Pass (5-6) Very good (7-8) Excellent (9-10)
    R1. Structure and files CA1.1 One of the 4 pages is missing, a skeleton is incomplete (no doctype, lang, charset or title) or a page does not have its own title. The 4 pages with a complete skeleton and their own title in each one. Also, headings in order (one h1 per page, no skipped levels) and file and folder names in lower case, with no spaces or accents. Also, consistent indentation and comments that separate the parts of each page.
    R2. Lists, links, images and table CA1.2 The menu is not the same or fails in some page, there are fewer than 5 images in images/, some image has no descriptive alt, or the table or the list is missing. The same menu in the 4 pages with every link working; at least 5 images in images/ with a descriptive alt; at least one table and one list. Also, a table with a header row in th and external links that open in a new tab. Also, a caption in the table or at least one figure with figcaption.
    R3. Contact form CA1.3 Fewer than 6 fields, a field without label, or no compulsory field with required. 6 fields, all with label, and required in the compulsory ones. Also, every label linked with for/id and at least 3 input types that fit the content (e.g. email, tel, select, textarea). Also, fields grouped with fieldset and legend, and consistent validation (type, minlength).
    R4. Semantics and validation CA1.4 One of the tags header, nav, main, section or footer is missing in some page, or the validator gives more than 3 errors adding up the 4 pages. header, nav, main, section and footer in the 4 pages, and at most 3 errors in the validator adding up the 4 pages. Also, 0 errors in the validator and one section for each block of content, with its own heading. Also, article for independent content (e.g. each project) and div only for layout.
    R5. Selectors, text and colours CA2.1 Some style inside the HTML (style= or <style>), more than one style sheet, or no Google Font. One single external style sheet linked from the 4 pages, no style inside the HTML and a Google Font applied. Also, class and descendant selectors (not only tag selectors), line height of 1.5 or more and readable text on every background. Also, a scheme of 3-4 colours applied the same way in the whole site, with no repeated or contradictory rules.
    R6. Box model CA2.2 No box-sizing: border-box, content stuck to the edges, or blocks that overlap. box-sizing: border-box; all the content separated from the edges with padding or margin, and no overlapping blocks. Also, a centred container with a maximum width and at least one block with border-radius and box-shadow. Also, the same spacing in equivalent elements of the 4 pages.
    R7. Flexbox and grid CA2.3 The menu does not use flexbox, or the projects page uses neither grid nor flexbox (e.g. it is laid out with tables or spaces). Menu with flexbox and projects page with grid or flexbox, with no overlapping elements. Also, the menu aligned with justify-content, align-items and gap, and the project cards aligned, with images of the same height. Also, in your presentation you explain why you chose flexbox or grid in each case.
    R8. Responsive design CA2.4 No viewport, no media query, or at 375 px there is horizontal scroll or content cut off. viewport, at least one media query and, at 375 px, no horizontal scroll and no content cut off. Also, correct at 768 and 1200 px, and the menu and the projects change their layout depending on the width. Also, written mobile first (min-width).
    R9. Good practice, delivery and presentation CA2.5 Fewer than 5 variables in :root, no :hover with transition, incomplete zip or README, late delivery, or a presentation without the thing you are proud of or the one you would improve. :root with at least 5 variables; at least one :hover with transition; zip with the correct name and a complete README; a 3-minute presentation with one thing you are proud of and one you would improve. Also, all the colours in variables and the CSS divided into commented sections. Also, you answer well the teacher's question about your code.
